How the lead-quality calculator works

The key difference is between raw enquiries and qualified leads. A raw form fill could be unrelated, unresponsive or outside the service area. A qualified lead should satisfy your documented buying-fit criteria. This calculator treats a qualified lead and a closed outcome as distinct events, broadly consistent with Google Ads' qualified and converted lead definitions.

Formula glossary

  • Total campaign cost = ad spend + management fee + other entered marketing costs.
  • Cost per lead (CPL) = total campaign cost / unique leads.
  • Cost per qualified lead (CPQL) = total campaign cost / qualified leads.
  • Cost per attended appointment = total campaign cost / attended appointments or meetings.
  • Simplified customer acquisition cost = total campaign cost / closed customers. Sales-team cost is excluded unless entered.
  • Qualified lead rate = qualified leads / leads × 100; lead-to-close rate = closed customers / leads × 100.
  • Return on ad spend (ROAS) = revenue / ad spend. This is not profit.
  • Estimated contribution = closed customers × (revenue per customer − variable cost per customer) − campaign cost. It is not net profit, and excludes tax and unentered overhead.

How to avoid misleading ROI comparisons

Keep the time period consistent, but remember that some leads close weeks or months after the original click. Comparing this month's new leads with this month's sales may mix different cohorts. If you have CRM data, use the same original lead cohort for all four counts. Deduplicate contacts and do not add website clicks, calls and form submissions together without checking overlap. Paid ad ROAS should use revenue attributable to the ads rather than total business revenue.

Do not compare an IVF appointment funnel, an apartment site-visit funnel and an industrial RFQ funnel against the same supposed standard. Use your own baseline from a comparable date range and track changes after introducing a new campaign or service page. Where a process has no meeting stage, enter 0 for attended appointments; that particular metric will be undefined, but overall leads-to-sales conversion can still be analysed.

What should you improve next?

If lots of raw leads fail qualification, investigate keyword targeting, offers, location relevance and form wording. If qualified leads rarely attend meetings, improve follow-up speed, booking reminders and expectation setting. If attended meetings are strong but sales are weak, examine product fit, pricing, service availability and sales processes. If ROAS looks high but contribution is negative, don't expand budget before checking margins and direct costs.
