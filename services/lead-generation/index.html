QUALIFIED LEAD GENERATION

Design the funnel beyond the first contact

Lead generation should produce useful sales conversations, not only large lists of names or form submissions. The right funnel is different for a hospital appointment, a property site visit and a B2B quotation. Before investing in a channel, define what qualifies an enquiry and how the business will respond.

What a focused engagement covers

  1. Lead definition and source mapping: Agree what counts as a unique lead, a qualified lead and a downstream outcome. Treat repeat calls, directions, spam and clicks as separate metrics where appropriate.
  2. Conversion journey: Align channels, landing pages, calls, forms, WhatsApp and sales handoff. Show relevant qualification information without collecting unnecessary sensitive data.
  3. Follow-up ownership: Recommend clear lead stages, acknowledgement expectations and reporting responsibilities. A CRM can help, but the operational process matters more than the tool name.
  4. Funnel analysis: Compare cost per enquiry with cost per qualified lead, attended meeting, site visit or sale. Make any gaps and assumptions explicit before attributing revenue.

How to decide if this is the right service

Begin with the business problem and the available data. We assess existing assets and the enquiry journey before recommending a standalone engagement or a connected project.

Best next step: share the target audience, locations or markets, current enquiry sources, approval constraints and one business outcome you want to improve.

Scope and pricing: complexity, locations, existing assets, implementation needs and review responsibilities affect a proposal; we do not publish fabricated universal pricing or guarantee rankings or leads.

Frequently asked questions

How is a qualified lead different from a lead?
A lead is an enquiry or contact record. A qualified lead meets agreed fit and intent criteria; those criteria should be tailored to your service.

Do you guarantee a fixed number of customers?
No. Outcomes depend on budgets, offer strength, sales response, competition and other factors outside an agency's control.

Can this work with our existing CRM?
Often yes, subject to reviewing the current tools and access. Tracking and follow-up requirements are defined during scoping.
