Real Estate Project Launch Marketing: From Readiness to Site Visits

A property launch is not only an advertising campaign. Accurate project information, an accessible buyer journey and a responsive sales process must be ready before paid traffic begins.

Verify the offer before announcing it

Collect the approved project name, developer, exact location, relevant disclosures, registration details where required, current configurations, sanctioned creative and legitimate pricing information. Have the authorised team check claims before any advert is published.

Avoid fabricated possession dates, promises of appreciation or unsupported local infrastructure claims. Buyers need reliable information more than a dramatic countdown banner.

Give people a landing page worth visiting

A project page should state the verified location, available unit types, relevant specifications, approved imagery and contact options. The mobile version and enquiry route should be tested on real devices before launch.

The page should tell a buyer what happens after submitting an enquiry, including whether sales will call and how a site visit can be requested. A generic contact form with no project context can leave a sales team guessing.

Select channels by actual buyer stage

Search can capture active project and location interest. Social advertising may introduce a development to people still comparing options. Relevant organic content helps some buyers research over a longer period.

Not every launch needs all channels immediately. Begin with an approach the sales team can monitor, add capacity when the initial enquiries are relevant and keep every ad promise aligned with the landing page.

Plan the operational handoff

Define who responds to phone, form and messaging enquiries, how quickly a suitable prospect is acknowledged and who confirms a visit. Keep the project identifier and source with the lead record wherever possible.

A pilot can reveal budget mismatches, duplicate enquiries and unanswered questions. These should drive changes to content, campaign settings or follow-up, not simply a demand for more leads.

Review completed visits and verified bookings

Track raw enquiries, reachable buyers, qualified prospects, site visits scheduled, visits attended and bookings as different stages. Marketing dashboards should not claim a booking merely because a visit has been requested.

Document inventory changes and remove outdated offers promptly. Budget planning should be based on the project's actual economics and capacity, not a guaranteed industry benchmark.

Frequently asked questions

What needs approval before launch?

Real project details, pricing statements, creative claims, required disclosures and the enquiry process.

Must a project launch use SEO and paid ads together?

No. The right channel mix follows demand, budget and available execution capacity.

When is it sensible to scale advertising?

When valid buyer interest, sales response and project availability justify more demand.
