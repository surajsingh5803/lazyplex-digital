Google Business Profile for Doctors: Eligibility and Local SEO Checklist

A doctor, a hospital and a hospital department may all serve patients, but they are not interchangeable listings. Before making SEO changes, establish which public-facing professional or location qualifies and how a person can contact it.

The difference between a hospital and practitioner listing

A hospital profile represents a legitimate organisation and location. An individual practitioner profile represents a public-facing professional who meets Google's specific criteria. A doctor should be directly contactable at the verified location during stated hours. A department may qualify separately in some cases, but not merely because a marketing team wants another ranking opportunity.

Google also says practitioners should not create several profiles solely to represent different specialisations. Profiles should reflect real arrangements, not campaign keywords. If multiple doctors practise within one hospital, the hospital and each eligible practitioner may require different ownership, verification and landing-page decisions.

Source or next step: Google practitioner and department guidance.

Audit every existing profile before adding new ones

Start with a register of the hospital, branches, departments and doctors. Record the exact verified name, address, phone, website or appointment link, relevant categories, consultation hours and the person authorised to make changes. Check whether each practitioner is truly available and publicly contactable at that location.

A consultant's old hospital listing can become misleading after a move. Fixing the address and booking route may do more for patients than publishing a new keyword-heavy profile. Do not create a new branch or speciality listing without confirming eligibility and useful differentiation.

Make the attached website page useful

An eligible practitioner profile should lead to a useful doctor page, not a generic directory where the visitor must search again. Include approved qualifications, actual consultation locations, broad service scope and a clear appointment route. Ensure people using mobile phones can call or request the appropriate consultation easily.

Clinical treatment descriptions and claims still require authorised medical review. Search optimisation can improve structure and wording, but should not invent awards, qualifications or patient outcomes. A hospital with several specialities also needs internal links between department information, valid doctor profiles and actual locations.

Source or next step: Doctor marketing service.

Measure local discovery without manufacturing patient counts

Google Business Profile actions such as calls, direction requests and website clicks describe interactions with listings, not necessarily unique patients. A visitor might request directions, call later and submit a website form. Those actions should remain separate columns in reporting rather than automatically being counted as three leads.

Where reception staff can confirm valid consultation requests in aggregate, compare them with source trends. Avoid passing medical details into tracking tools. Always note limitations when a profile's platform counts cannot be reconciled with booking records.

Source or next step: Lead counting guide.

A practical four-step review

First, verify real locations and practitioner eligibility. Second, check profile accuracy and direct contact options. Third, align each listing with an accurate, accessible website page. Fourth, document profile actions and appointment enquiries using separate definitions.

Revisit these details when doctors change schedules or hospital branches change services. Eligibility and useful patient discovery are ongoing operational responsibilities, not one-time keyword optimisation tasks.

Frequently asked questions

Can every hospital doctor have a GBP listing?

No. Eligibility depends on the practitioner's public-facing role and direct contactability, among other requirements.

Should doctors create profiles for every treatment?

No. Google's published guidance does not permit multiple practitioner profiles simply for specialisations.

Do direction requests count as new patients?

No. Directions are a navigation-interest metric; they are not verified patient enquiries.
