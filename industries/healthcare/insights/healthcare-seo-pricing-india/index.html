Healthcare SEO Pricing in India: How to Compare Agency Proposals

A five-page clinic website and a multi-branch hospital with hundreds of practitioner, service and location pages do not need identical SEO scopes. A reliable comparison is based on work, approvals and outcomes—not on an unsupported national monthly price claim.

What creates the actual workload

A healthcare SEO budget is shaped by website size, technical condition, branch complexity, current search visibility, CMS access, accuracy of existing medical information and number of pages needing clinical approval. The effort to write a clinically verified service explanation can differ sharply from editing a routine navigation label.

A hospital group may also need doctor profile governance, redirects for outdated pages and cooperation between technical staff, marketing managers and authorised clinical reviewers. One proposal may include implementation while another only supplies an audit; their quoted prices cannot be compared without adjusting for scope.

Compare three different kinds of engagement

A foundation project establishes indexation, a keyword-to-URL map, priority technical fixes and a reliable reporting baseline. A focused programme might optimise selected verified departments and practitioner pages each month. A larger multi-location engagement may also maintain location governance, content reviews and repeatable publishing processes.

These describe possible scopes, not fixed products or guaranteed performance. We deliberately do not publish a “typical Indian SEO fee” without credible comparable market data. Ad spend, hosting, specialist photography, paid tools and clinician review should be included or excluded explicitly in a written agreement.

Demand a concrete deliverables list

Ask which pages are included, which technical fixes will be implemented, who approves clinical wording and who owns development changes. Request clear reporting definitions, a review cadence and the process for correcting medically inaccurate information.

A high word count or a promise to target hundreds of keywords is not evidence of quality. Existing pages may need consolidation rather than expansion. The buyer should know whether the service is responsible for publishing content or only preparing drafts.

Make measurement part of the quotation

Search Console can report impressions, clicks and average query positions, but none directly proves that a patient booked or attended a consultation. A scoped engagement should separate these signals from website contact actions and any validated operational enquiry stages the hospital can share.

If the goal is better non-branded discovery for priority service lines, the agreement should define those pages and queries. Where clinic staff cannot connect online actions to appointments, the report must state the gap instead of asserting revenue from unverified conversions.

Questions to put to the agency before signing

Ask what problem the agency would fix first, what evidence supports that priority and what it will not guarantee. Clarify the frequency of site changes, handling of rankings fluctuations, content review responsibilities and what happens if clinical approval delays publication.

A sensible first phase may optimise the strongest existing department pages and improve navigation before creating another 20 articles. Demand a plan that fits actual service availability, site condition and staff capacity.

Frequently asked questions

What is the monthly price of hospital SEO?

There is no reliable universal fee; request an itemised scope reflecting your website, locations and approval needs.

Is 3,000 words automatically better than 700?

No. Content should be complete and helpful for its purpose, without padding.

Are paid advertising costs included?

Only if the written proposal says so. Ads, development and third-party tools are separate line items unless explicitly included.
